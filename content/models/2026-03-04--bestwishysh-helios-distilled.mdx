Helios is a 14B diffusion video model in the Wan2.1 family that’s positioned as “real-time long video generation.” The authors claim it can generate minute-scale clips with strong coherence while reaching high end-to-end throughput (they report 19.5 FPS on a single H100) without leaning on the usual acceleration bag of tricks (KV cache, sparse attention, TinyVAE, quantization, etc.).

Even if you don’t fully buy the headline numbers, the framing is interesting: instead of optimizing only the sampler, Helios emphasizes training + inference changes that reduce memory pressure and keep generation stable over longer horizons. That’s a practical theme for anyone trying to ship video generation where latency and drift both matter.

What to try first: run the provided Diffusers pipeline with `num_frames` set to a multiple of 33 (the model generates in 33-frame chunks), then compare a short prompt vs. a longer, “story-like” prompt to see how well identity and motion stay consistent across multiple chunks.
