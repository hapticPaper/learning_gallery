ACE-V1.1 is a YOLOv11 (`ultralytics`) fine-tune intended for **brain tumor detection in MRI scans**. The model card links to an associated BRISC 2025 dataset/paper (arXiv:2506.14318) and includes practical “how to run it” details: it’s trained for 640×640 inputs and the author recommends a confidence threshold of 0.466.

The card also reports evaluation numbers (e.g., mAP50 ≈ 0.9 on a held-out test, plus a claimed **1.00 “background specificity”** focused on reducing false positives in healthy tissue). Treat these as *author-reported metrics* rather than a guarantee — especially for medical imaging, the biggest risk in practice is domain shift (scanner differences, protocols, demographics, labeling standards). The weights are shared under **CC-BY-NC-4.0**, so even if it performs well, usage is constrained to non-commercial contexts.

What to try first: run it on a small labeled MRI set you trust, tune the confidence threshold for your desired false-positive rate, and inspect failure cases manually (missed small lesions vs spurious detections). If you’re considering any clinical workflow integration, treat this as research and validate it through your institution’s normal review process.
