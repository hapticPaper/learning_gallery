This is a reminder that the “AI trade” isn’t just model labs — it’s an entire stack of companies that can benefit (or be disrupted) as AI adoption shows up in capex budgets, enterprise software spend, and consumer product roadmaps.

The piece highlights three publicly traded companies tied to AI momentum, and frames them through Wall Street analysts’ upside estimates for 2026. The useful part isn’t the exact target numbers (those can change fast), but the reasoning patterns: what revenue streams are assumed to accelerate, whether the business has pricing power as AI becomes table stakes, and how much the thesis depends on sustained infrastructure buildout vs. one-time catch-up.

Taken as a checklist, it’s a good prompt to look past headline “AI exposure” and ask harder questions: is the company selling scarce capacity (compute, chips, data center services), selling software seats that become sticky with AI features, or simply re-rating on hype? And what would have to be true for the upside case to hold — continued AI spend, improving margins, and a defensible moat once competitors copy the obvious features.
